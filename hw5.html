Welford's recurrences and sampling statistics

The mean, the sum of squares and the variance of a sequence of scores can be updated one value at a time. These recurrences are used to compute, for every hacker, the sampling mean and the sampling variance of the scores obtained on n systems, and to compare them with the mean and variance of the score distribution.

1 Welford's recurrence formulas

For the first k values x₁, …, xₖ let Mₖ = (1/k)·Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ xᵢ be the mean, SSₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ (xᵢ − Mₖ)² the sum of squares and sₖ² = SSₖ/(k − 1) the sampling variance, with M₀ = SS₀ = 0.

Knuth's recurrence for the mean

The sum of the first k values is the sum of the first k − 1 plus the new one:

  1. k·Mₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ⁻¹ xᵢ + xₖ = (k − 1)·Mₖ₋₁ + xₖ
  2. k·Mₖ = k·Mₖ₋₁ + (xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)
  3. Mₖ = Mₖ₋₁ + (xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)/k

Welford's recurrence for the sum of squares

The same idea is applied to SSₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ xᵢ² − k·Mₖ²: the sum of the squares grows only by xₖ², so

  1. SSₖ − SSₖ₋₁ = xₖ² − k·Mₖ² + (k − 1)·Mₖ₋₁²
  2. from the mean recurrence (k − 1)·Mₖ₋₁ = k·Mₖ − xₖ, hence (k − 1)·Mₖ₋₁² = k·Mₖ·Mₖ₋₁ − xₖ·Mₖ₋₁
  3. SSₖ − SSₖ₋₁ = xₖ·(xₖ − Mₖ₋₁) − k·Mₖ·(Mₖ − Mₖ₋₁)
  4. from the mean recurrence k·(Mₖ − Mₖ₋₁) = xₖ − Mₖ₋₁, hence SSₖ − SSₖ₋₁ = (xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)·(xₖ − Mₖ)
  5. SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + (xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)·(xₖ − Mₖ)

Recurrence for the variance

Dividing by the degrees of freedom, (k − 1)·sₖ² = (k − 2)·sₖ₋₁² + (xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)·(xₖ − Mₖ), which has the same form as the mean recurrence, for k ≥ 2 (with s₁² = 0, since SS₁ = 0):

  1. sₖ² = sₖ₋₁² + [(xₖ − Mₖ₋₁)·(xₖ − Mₖ) − sₖ₋₁²]/(k − 1)

Each new value needs only the current k, Mₖ₋₁ and SSₖ₋₁: one pass over the data and constant memory. Unlike Σxᵢ² − k·Mₖ², the update never subtracts two large and almost equal numbers, so it does not lose precision.

2 Sampling mean and sampling variance

Each penetration attempt receives a score between 0 and 10. The score is X = 10·B with B ~ Beta(α = 2, β = 5), whose density is f(x) = 3·(x/10)·(1 − x/10)⁴ on [0, 10]: most attempts get a low score and high scores are rare, as when only a few systems are seriously compromised. A value of B is drawn as the second smallest of six independent uniform numbers on [0, 1].

Each of the m hackers attacks n systems and gets n independent scores. Welford's recurrences (section 1) give the hacker's sampling mean x̄ and sampling variance s² = SSₙ/(n − 1); then x̄ and s² are averaged over the m hackers.

3 Different numbers of systems n

The experiment is repeated with the same m hackers of section 2 for n = 2, 5, 10, 30, 100 and 1000 systems. For each n the table compares the spread of x̄ and s² across hackers with the theory: sd(x̄) = σ/√n and sd(s²) = σ²·√(κ/n − (n − 3)/(n(n − 1))), where κ = E[(X − μ)⁴]/σ⁴ = 2.88 for this distribution.

Conclusions

  • The averages match the theory. For every n, the average of the sampling means is close to μ and the average of the sampling variances is close to σ². The sampling mean and the sampling variance with divisor n − 1 are unbiased estimators: E[x̄] = μ and E[s²] = σ². With divisor n the average would be (n − 1)/n·σ², too small, clearly visible for n = 2 where it would be half of σ².
  • Why it is useful in inferential statistics. In practice μ and σ² are unknown and only one sample is observed, here the n scores of one hacker. Unbiasedness guarantees that x̄ and s² computed from the sample are correct on average, with no systematic error. s² also estimates the uncertainty of x̄, through the standard error s/√n, which is what confidence intervals and tests are built on.
  • Effect of n. The number of systems does not move the centre of the estimators, but it reduces their variability: the standard deviations of x̄ and s² across hackers decrease like 1/√n, so the histograms narrow around μ and σ². With few systems a single hacker's estimate can be far from the true value, especially for the variance, whose distribution is skewed for small n; with many systems every hacker's estimates are close to μ and σ².
  • Effect of m. The number of hackers does not change the variability of a single hacker's estimates: it only makes their averages, and the histograms, more precise, with an error that decreases like 1/√m.
